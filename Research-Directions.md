# Research Directions

## Central doctoral problem

The SEFI–DEFI–GWFM programme asks whether explicit nonlinear field models support localized persistent excitations and whether their perturbations admit compatible effective propagation geometries. Primordial unity supplies motivation, not a premise. SEFI identity-space, DEFI variational coupling, and GWFM worldline constructions remain subject to analysis and comparison with established physics.

## Primary direction: localized excitations and effective geometry

The [23 September 2026 checkpoint](10_DISSERTATION/research_direction_2026_09_23/research_direction_2026_09_23.md) records finite-domain traveling vortex rings, continuous global Noether charge, limited perturbation tests, two preliminary non-axisymmetric probes, and a low-energy phase-sector effective metric. It also records a Derrick-type obstruction under specified rest-state assumptions and unsuccessful alternative rest-loop searches.

Priority work is continuum and domain convergence, existence and nonexistence analysis, conserved quantities, spectral analysis across azimuthal sectors, nonlinear three-dimensional evolution, dimensional calibration, and comparison of characteristic geometries across sectors. Finite-grid residuals and finite-duration persistence establish neither continuum existence nor general stability.

## Displacement-engineered warp geometry

The current manuscript is *Displacement-Engineered Warp Fields: Stability Surfaces and Worldline Geometry* (29 September 2026). Its proposed objects are

$$
D:\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^4,\qquad
C(D,\nabla D,\nabla^2D,I^a)=0,
$$

$$
\mathcal E(D)=\{x\mid C=0\},\qquad
\Omega_D=\nabla\times D,\qquad
\dot\gamma=D(\gamma),\qquad
\dot I^a=f(D).
$$

The manuscript reports numerical envelope surfaces, a radial residual, vortex structure, and guided worldline deformation. The spatial curl and suppressed identity-space argument require explicit implementation conventions. The envelope is a candidate constraint set, not an established stable region.

Next work must specify an action or governing equations, explicit and dimensionally consistent $C$ and $f$, boundary conditions, numerical convergence, and perturbative stability. Physical interpretations additionally require derived characteristic geometry, stress-energy, causality, and comparison with relativistic dynamics. Figure-caption and prose inconsistencies are documented in [Warp-Field Geometry](Warp-Field-Geometry.md).

## Photonic quantum error correction

Investigate explicit stabilizer-to-geometry mappings, decoder construction, logical-error benchmarking, scaling, hardware-aware noise, and ablations against established bosonic or photonic methods. Resemblance between algebraic stabilizers and geometric surfaces is a hypothesis requiring quantitative validation.

## Systems modeling

Investigate invariant manifolds, coupled state spaces, displacement and restoration maps, and geometric error measures in distributed dynamical systems. Every transfer requires domain-specific equations, assumptions, and baseline comparisons; geometry alone does not establish applicability.

## DNA handoff

Helical worldline and Frenet–Serret descriptions remain a specialist biophysics handoff. Biological interpretation, molecular modeling, and empirical validation are delegated; DNA geometry is not required to validate the central field theory.

## Evidence and reproducibility standard

Distinguish assumptions, analytical derivations, numerical observations, conjectures, failed searches, and experimental validation. Retain existing equations, profiles, residuals, perturbation histories, negative results, manuscripts, code, and submission records. Model changes require versioned derivations and suitable regression and convergence checks.

No present result establishes observed matter, spin or quantum statistics, electromagnetic identification of model charge, universal coupling, Einstein gravity, physical warp propulsion, continuum existence, or general stability. Quantization, common effective geometry, and gravitational dynamics remain separate longer-term research problems.
